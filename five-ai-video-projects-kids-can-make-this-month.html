Five AI Video Projects Kids Can Make This Month

Simple creative challenges that teach storytelling, planning, and responsible AI use.

Lux Automaton EditorialJuly 16, 20265 min read
01Start with a storyboard 02Use short scenes 03Explain what the AI helped create

AI video becomes less mysterious when kids build in small scenes. One character, one setting, one action, and one feeling are enough for a strong first project.

Try a neighborhood helper robot, a weather report from the future, a kindness commercial, a science explainer, or a mini adventure where a character solves a real problem.

The important lesson is not just the final video. Students learn to plan, revise, credit tools, check facts, protect privacy, and explain their choices.

Ace asks: “What do you think your story should help someone understand, feel, or do?”

01. Start with a storyboard

A storyboard is a simple map of the video. Kids do not need advanced drawing skills. Four boxes are enough:

  1. Character: Who is the story about?
  2. Setting: Where does the scene happen?
  3. Action: What does the character do?
  4. Ending: What changes or gets solved?

Planning first helps children make creative decisions before a tool starts generating options. It also makes it easier to notice when an AI result does not match the original idea.

One character + one setting + one action + one feeling

Before a child uploads a photo, voice recording, or personal story, a trusted adult should review the tool’s privacy rules and age requirements. Use original characters whenever possible, and ask permission before including another person’s face, voice, name, artwork, or experience.

Five projects to try

1. Neighborhood Helper Robot

Create a friendly robot that notices a small community problem and helps solve it. The robot might organize a cleanup, water a garden, or help neighbors find a lost item.

Skills: empathy · cause and effect · character motivation

Responsible AI checkpoint: Keep addresses, school names, and identifiable neighborhood details private.

2. Weather Report From the Future

Become a weather reporter in the year 2050. Research real weather terms, invent future technology, and clearly label predictions as imagination.

Skills: research · presentation · world-building

Responsible AI checkpoint: Check weather and climate claims with trusted sources before presenting them as facts.

3. Kindness Commercial

Make a 15–30 second message that inspires one action: include someone, thank a helper, protect a shared space, or speak respectfully online.

Skills: persuasive writing · tone · audience awareness

Responsible AI checkpoint: Use original characters or people who gave permission. Never embarrass someone or reveal a private situation.

4. Science in 60 Seconds

Answer one focused question with a beginning, one main idea, and a simple recap. Ask AI for examples or visual metaphors, then verify every important fact.

Skills: research · simplification · teaching

Responsible AI checkpoint: For health topics, AI is a learning assistant—not a substitute for a qualified healthcare professional.

5. Mini Adventure—Solve It!

Create a character with a goal, an obstacle, and a helpful idea. The character can ask for help, revise the plan, and learn from a mistake.

Skills: problem-solving · revision · emotional growth

Responsible AI checkpoint: Avoid copying famous characters or imitating living artists. Original worlds help children develop their own voices.

02. Use short scenes

Long prompts often ask the AI to manage too many details at once. Short scenes are easier to plan, generate, review, and replace. For a first project, try three to five clips of about five to eight seconds each. Give every clip one job.

Character + setting + action + feeling + camera view

When the next scene begins, repeat the important character details. Kids can keep a “character recipe” with clothing, colors, size, personality, and special features. This improves continuity and teaches careful observation.

Does it match the storyboard?
Is the action easy to understand?
Did the character stay consistent?
Is anything unsafe or misleading?
Should we keep it or revise it?
Did we check important facts?

Revision is not a failure. It is the work of directing.

03. Explain what the AI helped create

A responsible creator can explain where the idea came from, what the AI generated, and what the human changed.

“I created the idea and storyboard. I used an AI tool to help make visual options. I selected the clips, changed the script, checked the facts, and completed the final edit.”

A project credit card might include:

  • Story idea: Me
  • Storyboard: Me, with feedback from a parent, teacher, Ace, or LANA
  • Images or clips: AI-assisted
  • Facts: Checked with trusted sources
  • Voice: My narration or a properly licensed voice
  • Final decisions and edit: Me

LANA’s reminder is simple: AI can suggest, generate, and organize. People still choose the purpose, judge the result, protect others, and decide what should be shared.

A one-month LUX AI Kids challenge

Week 1: Storyboard the Neighborhood Helper Robot.

Week 2: Create the Future Weather Report and Kindness Commercial.

Week 3: Research and produce Science in 60 Seconds.

Week 4: Build the Mini Adventure, then host a small showcase.

At the showcase, ask: What decision are you most proud of? What did AI help create? What did you change, verify, or improve yourself?

The real project is becoming a thoughtful creator

A finished video is exciting, but the deeper win is the process. Kids learn that a strong story starts with a plan, that short scenes are easier to direct, that AI output needs human review, and that honest creators explain their tools.

Mistakes become clues. Revision becomes normal. Creativity becomes connected to consent, accuracy, and care.
